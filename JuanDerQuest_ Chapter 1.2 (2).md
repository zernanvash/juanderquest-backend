**INTRODUCTION**  
**Background of the Study**

Tourism has long been recognized as a powerful catalyst for economic development and cultural preservation across the globe. I                                                                                                                                                                                     pite this importance, the promotion of tourist destinations in the country, particularly in. provincial areas such as Pangasinan, continues to face a range of unresolved structural and operational challenges that prevent the industry from reaching its full potential. First, many local governmq	ent units still rely on manual, paper-based documentation to record tourist visits and manage destination data, resulting in inaccurate visitor statistics and inefficient tourism planning (Rodrigues et al., 2023). Second, tourism revenues are often managed through centralized and opaque fund systems, leaving community members unable to verify how collected funds are allocated or spent, which erodes public trust and diminishes community participation (Pascual et al., 2024). Third, existing tourism promotion strategies lack mechanisms that actively engage and reward tourists for visiting destinations, resulting in low visitor retention and weak destination loyalty (Pasca et al., 2021). Fourth, the absence of transparent and tamper-proof reward systems means that tourists who participate in promotional activities receive no verifiable proof of their contributions or incentives, weakening motivation for sustained engagement (Rana et al., 2022). Lastly, local communities, particularly residents of tourist-receiving barangays, are largely excluded from decision-making processes concerning tourism development and fund disbursement, undermining the long-term sustainability of community-based tourism initiatives (Lapuz, 2023). These problems motivated the researchers to develop JuanDerQuest: a gamified blockchain-based system designed to address the gaps in transparent reward distribution, community participation, and digital tourism promotion.

Digital technology has become the primary medium through which tourism destinations are discovered, marketed, and experienced by travelers worldwide. Buhalis et al. (2023) established that smart tourism technologies — including mobile applications, Internet of Things (IoT) sensors, artificial intelligence (AI), and big data analytics — have substantially transformed destination management by enabling real-time, data-driven decisions that improve both tourist experiences and operational efficiency. Specifically, AI-powered recommendation engines deployed across tourism-dependent economies in Asia have demonstrated measurable increases in destination engagement, tourist satisfaction, and revisit intention, illustrating how digital technology amplifies promotional reach and conversion (Madzík et al., 2023). Lapuz (2023) confirmed that the persistence of non-digital operational practices in Philippine provincial tourism offices perpetuates limited destination reach, weak community engagement, and the exclusion of grassroots stakeholders from governance decisions that directly affect their economic wellbeing. The gap between tourism's documented economic significance and the operational limitations of manual promotion methods reveals a structural mismatch between Pangasinan's considerable tourism potential and its limited capacity to promote, monitor, and equitably govern that potential. These developments indicate that technology adoption has become increasingly important for tourism destinations seeking sustainable growth, particularly in emerging economies like the Philippines where digitalization can address critical promotional infrastructure gaps.  
Blockchain technology is a decentralized, distributed ledger system in which data is recorded across a network of computers in cryptographically secured, immutable blocks, such that no single party can alter a record without network-wide consensus (Rana et al., 2022). Each block contains a cryptographic hash of the previous block, a timestamp, and transaction data, forming a tamper-resistant audit chain that enables any stakeholder to independently verify any record without relying on a central administrator. Zarrin et al. (2021) demonstrated that blockchain's core properties — decentralization, transparency, and immutability — make it structurally superior to conventional databases in multi-stakeholder environments where no single party should serve as the unchecked record-keeper. Baydeniz (2024) further established that blockchain's smart contract functionality enables the automated enforcement of agreements and the generation of verifiable proof of transactions, directly addressing the accountability deficits that plague conventional fund management and certification systems across service-based industries including tourism. These properties collectively position blockchain as a foundational infrastructure for building institutional trust in multi-stakeholder platforms such as community-based tourism systems, where transparency and auditability are essential conditions for equitable governance.  
Web3 represents the third evolutionary phase of the internet, defined by decentralization, user data ownership, and trustless peer-to-peer interactions enabled by blockchain technology and smart contracts rather than centralized platform intermediaries  (Zarrin et al., 2021). In Web3 systems, platform rules and data governance are distributed across a network of participants through smart contracts and decentralized autonomous organizations (DAOs), eliminating the dependency on commercial intermediaries whose interests may not align with community welfare. Calzada (2024) demonstrated that Web3 architectures enable decentralized community governance through token-based democratic mechanisms, where stakeholders participate directly in platform decisions rather than being subject to the unilateral choices of platform owners. Heidari and Hosseinalibeiki (2025) further argued that integrating Web3 token economies with user-facing applications closes the intention-action gap in sustainable behaviors by converting abstract commitments into concrete, immediately rewarded actions permanently recorded on an immutable public ledger. The emergence of Web3 therefore offers tourism systems a novel architectural paradigm in which community governance, transparent fund management, and reward distribution can be encoded as self-executing protocols, eliminating the opacity and accountability deficits that centralized tourism fund management systems consistently produce.

The application of blockchain technology to the tourism industry addresses several persistent operational problems, including identity fraud, revenue opacity, and the inability to independently verify sustainability claims made by destination operators or tourism fund administrators. Rana et al. (2022) conducted a systematic literature review of blockchain adoption in tourism and found that blockchain's immutability and transparency directly address trust deficits in booking verification, loyalty programs, and community fund management, enabling all stakeholders to audit transactions without reliance on a central authority. Balasubramanian et al. (2022) developed a blockchain enabling framework specifically for tourism and found that smart contract automation of service agreements produces measurable improvements in transaction reliability and stakeholder accountability across tourism supply chains. Baydeniz (2024) established that blockchain smart contracts enable decentralized management of community tourism funds, ensuring that revenues collected from tourists are distributed according to pre-agreed, publicly visible rules without the risk of elite capture or administrative mismanagement. Heidari and Hosseinalibeiki (2025) proposed a blockchain-integrated tourism engagement architecture in which tokenized incentives provide verifiable transparency and foster sustainable loyalty among eco-tourism participants, offering a concrete deployment model directly applicable to community-based tourism contexts in developing economies.

Gamification is the deliberate application of game design mechanics — including points, badges, leaderboards, progress bars, challenges, and narrative-driven quests — to non-game environments with the objective of increasing user motivation, engagement, and behavioral compliance (Pasca et al., 2021). The theoretical basis of gamification rests on Self-Determination Theory, which holds that sustained behavioral engagement requires the satisfaction of three core psychological needs: autonomy, competence, and relatedness, all of which well-designed gamified systems can activate through structured reward and progression mechanics. Aguiar-Castillo et al. (2023) demonstrated through field experimentation across three European cities that gamification applied to environmental tourism behaviors produces measurable habit formation among travelers, confirming that gamified interventions go beyond short-term motivation to create lasting behavioral change. Wittayakom et al. (2025) further demonstrated that gamified systems with dynamic, adaptive challenge structures sustain participant engagement significantly longer than static reward programs, because adaptive mechanics maintain perceived competence and novelty among users over extended periods. These documented benefits establish gamification as a rigorously validated behavioral design strategy with direct applicability to tourism promotion systems that aim to sustain visitor engagement and community participation over time.  
Within the tourism sector, gamification has been deployed across destination discovery platforms, heritage interpretation systems, and sustainable behavior promotion campaigns, with documented success in increasing visitor engagement, destination loyalty, and pro-environmental action. Pasca et al. (2021) synthesized 36 empirical studies on gamification in tourism and hospitality and identified sustainable behavior, visitor engagement, and edutainment as the three domains in which gamification produces the most consistent and replicable positive outcomes, confirming its role as a foundational design strategy for next-generation tourism platforms. Rosmadi et al. (2024) studied meaningful gamification in ecotourism and found that game elements centered on exposition, engagement, and reflection — delivered through a mobile platform — effectively foster positive ecotourism behavior by raising environmental awareness and motivating tourists to adopt sustainable practices in an interactive way. Fraga et al. (2025) conducted a systematic review of gamified sustainable urban tourism platforms and identified narrative eco-quests and real-time reward notifications as the most effective mechanics for translating tourist interest in sustainability into measurable behavioral change at physical destination sites. Wang and Huang (2025) additionally demonstrated that gamification grounded in Self-Determination Theory transforms passive tourists into active destination stewards, with gamified participants showing significantly higher revisit intention and willingness to recommend destinations compared to non-gamified control groups.  
The bodies of literature on technology, blockchain, Web3, and gamification converge on a shared insight: effective next-generation tourism promotion systems must move beyond passive information delivery toward architectures that are transparent, participatory, and behaviorally engaging by design. Rodrigues et al. (2023) articulate that the digitalization of tourism is most impactful when it simultaneously enables community empowerment, data-driven decision-making, and sustainable governance rather than serving merely as a promotional channel. Blockchain and Web3 technologies provide the trust infrastructure needed for transparent fund management and verifiable reward distribution, addressing the accountability deficits that manual and centralized digital systems cannot resolve at a structural level (Rana et al., 2022; Heidari & Hosseinalibeiki, 2025). Gamification supplies the human-centered engagement layer that converts passive destination visitors into active, repeat participants who derive tangible incentive value from their interactions, making sustained engagement a rational and rewarding choice rather than an act of civic goodwill (Pasca et al., 2021; Fraga et al., 2025). The critical synthesis is that no single layer is sufficient in isolation; it is their integration into a coherent, unified system that creates the conditions for equitable, transparent, and sustained community-based tourism promotion — which is the foundational design logic of JuanDerQuest.  
Despite the availability of digital tools, tourism promotion in many Philippine provincial contexts continues to depend on manual and traditional methods that are inadequate for the data demands and accountability standards of modern destination management. Common practices include the distribution of printed brochures and tarpaulin posters, the conduct of regional trade fairs and cultural festivals, and the recording of visitor arrivals in paper logbooks at destination entry points maintained by local government personnel. Rodrigues et al. (2023) documented that tourism offices relying on non-digital visitor recording systems systematically undercount arrivals and produce inaccurate destination statistics, which distort policy decisions and misallocate development resources that could otherwise generate equitable community benefit. Maravilla and Pelmin (2025) examined shared governance in community-oriented tourist destinations in the Philippines and found that its practical implementation is undermined by weak stakeholder engagement, lack of clear accountability structures, and the exclusion of community groups from meaningful decision-making, creating conditions that erode trust and discourage resident participation in tourism governance processes. Lapuz (2023) confirmed that the persistence of non-digital operational practices in Philippine provincial tourism offices perpetuates a cycle of limited destination reach, weak community engagement, and exclusion of grassroots stakeholders from governance decisions that directly affect their economic wellbeing and cultural identity.  
The gap between tourism's documented economic significance and the operational limitations of manual promotion methods in Philippine provincial settings reveals a systemic implementation deficit that requires a digitally grounded, community-centered policy response. While global tourism destinations have increasingly adopted smart tourism ecosystems integrating mobile platforms, real-time data analytics, and participatory digital tools, many local government units in the Philippines remain dependent on promotional strategies that cannot scale, cannot self-audit, and cannot engage contemporary tourists who expect seamless digital interaction with the destinations they visit (Rodrigues et al., 2023). The consequence is a structural mismatch between Pangasinan's considerable tourism potential — anchored in its diverse coastal ecosystems, heritage landmarks, and agricultural landscapes — and its limited capacity to promote, monitor, and equitably govern that potential through existing manual systems. Robina-Ramirez et al. (2021) argue that sustainable tourism development in the post-pandemic era requires governance systems that integrate digital accountability tools with meaningful community participation mechanisms, because manual systems are incapable of meeting the transparency and equity standards that both modern tourists and development partners now require. The digitalization of tourism promotion in Pangasinan is therefore not merely an operational upgrade but a foundational governance reform that is necessary for equitable and verifiably sustainable destination development.  
JuanDerQuest directly aligns with two specific targets of the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals that govern tourism's responsibilities in global sustainable development. SDG 8, Target 8.9 calls on governments to devise and implement policies to promote sustainable tourism that creates jobs and promotes local culture and products, generating equitable economic benefit for host communities rather than allowing gains to concentrate among large commercial intermediaries at the expense of the communities that bear the social and environmental costs of tourism (UNWTO, 2023). SDG 12, Target 12.b mandates the development and implementation of tools to monitor sustainable development impacts for sustainable tourism, requiring publicly accessible, verifiable mechanisms for tracking how tourism activity translates into measurable environmental and social outcomes (UNWTO, 2023). JuanDerQuest addresses Target 8.9 by creating token-based economic incentives for community members who actively promote and preserve Pangasinan's tourist destinations, generating a blockchain-verified livelihood pathway intrinsically linked to sustainable tourism activity rather than passive revenue extraction. Aligned with Target 12.b, the system develops and implements a concrete monitoring instrument through its immutable blockchain ledger, which permanently records tourist visits, quest completions, and fund disbursements in a format that is publicly auditable, tamper-proof, and independently verifiable by any stakeholder — capabilities that paper-based and centralized digital systems are structurally incapable of providing. The Philippine Development Plan 2023–2028 reinforces both targets by identifying digital innovation and sustainable tourism as complementary pillars of inclusive economic growth, situating JuanDerQuest firmly within a nationally mandated development framework (National Economic and Development Authority, 2023).   
Automation, in the context of digital information systems, refers to the use of software logic, algorithms, and network protocols to execute processes and transactions without continuous human intervention, enabling systems to operate with greater speed, accuracy, and scalability than manual processes allow (Buhalis et al., 2023). In tourism management, automation has been applied across online booking confirmation engines, dynamic pricing systems, visitor flow monitoring tools, and real-time destination performance dashboards that generate analytics impossible to produce manually at equivalent accuracy or speed. Automated smart tourism platforms that integrate IoT sensor data, mobile application inputs, and algorithmic processing have been shown to outperform manually managed systems in visitor satisfaction outcomes and the accuracy of destination analytics, providing a measurable performance advantage that justifies investment in digital automation infrastructure across tourism destinations (Buhalis et al., 2023). Smart contracts on blockchain platforms represent a specialized and particularly powerful form of automation in which predefined transactional rules are encoded into the blockchain and execute automatically when specified conditions are verified, eliminating manual approval steps and the human error, delay, and manipulation risks they introduce (Zarrin et al., 2021). The integration of smart contract automation with gamified quest engines is central to JuanDerQuest's ability to issue token rewards instantaneously upon GPS-verified quest completion, maintain a real-time engagement ledger, and enforce fund allocation rules without requiring administrative intermediaries whose involvement would reintroduce the opacity and inefficiency the system is designed to eliminate.  
Several documented systems and scholarly frameworks provide direct empirical and technical precedent for the core components integrated in JuanDerQuest. Balasubramanian et al. (2022) developed a blockchain enabling framework for tourism that established the feasibility of using distributed ledger technology to produce tamper-proof accountability records for community fund management and service delivery verification across multi-stakeholder tourism ecosystems. Srisomboon (2024) evaluated a gamified mobile ecotourism application in Thailand that used GPS-verified quest completion and digital badge rewards to sustain participation in eco-friendly destination activities, validating the quest-and-reward behavioral model that serves as JuanDerQuest's core engagement mechanism. Fraga et al. (2025) systematically reviewed gamified sustainable tourism platforms and identified the combination of geo-verified challenges, real-time reward notifications, and community leaderboards as the design configuration most consistently effective at producing sustained behavioral change among diverse tourist populations at physical destination sites. Erol et. al (2022) examined the challenges and opportunities of integrating blockchain technology into tourism sustainability frameworks and established that smart contract automation and decentralized reward distribution directly address the transparency and governance gaps left unresolved by conventional tourism management systems, offering a peer-reviewed technical precedent for JuanDerQuest's hybrid blockchain architecture. These related systems collectively validate the technical feasibility and behavioral effectiveness of JuanDerQuest's core components while confirming that no prior implementation has integrated blockchain transparency, gamified engagement, and DAO-based community governance within a single platform serving a Philippine provincial tourism context.  
JuanDerQuest is proposed as a direct and integrated response to the documented gaps in existing tourism promotion systems in Pangasinan and the broader Philippine provincial context, bringing three distinct contributions that no prior system has delivered in combination. First, the platform addresses the absence of transparent and verifiable fund management in community-based tourism by deploying blockchain infrastructure that records all transactions, quest completions, and fund disbursements in an immutable, publicly auditable ledger, ensuring that tourism revenues are distributed according to pre-agreed rules without the risk of elite capture or administrative mismanagement (Erol et al., 2022). Second, through its gamified quest system, tourists earn tokens for completing GPS-verified destination visits and community challenges, creating an incentive structure rooted in established behavioral science that directly rewards destination loyalty and pro-community action in ways that neither manual systems nor non-blockchain digital platforms are architecturally capable of replicating with verifiable, tamper-proof permanence (Fraga et al., 2025: Rosmadi et al., 2024). Third, the system's decentralized autonomous organization (DAO) governance module gives Pangasinan community members a blockchain-verified, democratic voice in how collected tourism funds are allocated to local conservation and development initiatives, operationalizing the community empowerment principles that Lapuz (2023) identifies as the decisive factor distinguishing sustainable from failed community-based tourism initiatives in the Philippine provincial context. JuanDerQuest therefore represents not merely a technological innovation but a governance reform instrument — one that makes tourism promotion in Pangasinan verifiably transparent, structurally participatory, and measurably aligned with both community wellbeing and internationally recognized sustainability standards that existing systems have proven incapable of meeting.  
The literature reviewed in this background establishes the necessity, technical feasibility, and strategic relevance of JuanDerQuest as a gamified blockchain-based tourism promotion system for Pangasinan. The persistent failures of manual tourism promotion — including opaque fund management, inaccurate visitor data, and the structural exclusion of grassroots stakeholders from governance — generate a well-documented demand for a transparent and participatory digital alternative (Maravilla & Pelmin, 2025; Lapuz, 2023; Rodrigues et al., 2023). Blockchain and Web3 technologies supply the trust infrastructure required to address accountability deficits at a structural level, while gamification mechanics provide the behavioral engagement framework that can sustain both tourist participation and community stewardship over time (Rana et al., 2022; Pasca et al., 2021; Erol et al., 2022). The system's alignment with SDG 8 Target 8.9 and SDG 12 Target 12.b, as well as the Philippine Development Plan 2023–2028 digitalization agenda, situates it within a recognized sustainability and governance framework relevant to Pangasinan's development context (UNWTO, 2023; National Economic and Development Authority, 2023). **Scope and Limitation of the Study**  
The capstone project will focus on the design, development, and implementation of JuanDerQuest, a gamified blockchain-based mobile application that will be developed to promote eco-tourism and cultural tourism destinations within the Province of Pangasinan. The application will serve as a digital platform that will encourage tourists and local residents to participate in sustainable tourism activities through location-based quests, cultural quizzes, and community engagement initiatives. Supported by a cloud-based backend infrastructure, the system will incorporate features such as location-based eco-quests, educational challenges, digital wallet integration, achievement tracking, community leaderboards, and a blockchain-powered reward mechanism. Through the Base Layer 2 network, the platform will automate the distribution of utility tokens using smart contracts and will issue non-transferable non-fungible token (NFT) achievement badges that will serve as permanent digital records of significant eco-tourism accomplishments. Furthermore, the system will accommodate three user roles: Users, who will participate in activities and receive rewards; Administrators, who will manage events, verify submissions, and oversee platform operations; and Merchants, who will provide incentives associated with token-based rewards.  
JuanDerQuest will not include direct fiat payment processing, online banking integration, or automated currency conversion services. The system will be entirely dependent on internet connectivity, as location verification, blockchain transactions, database synchronization, and reward distribution will require continuous network access, making offline operation unavailable. In addition, the blockchain reward mechanism will be subject to network transaction fees and congestion within the Base Layer 2 ecosystem, which may affect the efficiency of reward distribution. Due to the immutable nature of blockchain technology, deployed smart contracts will not be modifiable without undergoing a contract migration process. The accuracy of location-based verification will also depend on the capabilities of users' mobile devices and may be affected by GPS inaccuracies or geolocation spoofing techniques. Finally, the study will not cover comprehensive user education on blockchain wallet management, private key security, and other decentralized technology concepts, which may influence the adoption and effective utilization of the platform.

**Definition of Terms**  
	This section presents the definitions of key terms and concepts utilized throughout the study to establish a clear and consistent understanding of the terminology used in the project.  

**Base Layer 2 Network.** Conceptually, this refers to a secondary framework or protocol built on top of an existing blockchain network to increase transaction speed and reduce costs. Operationally, it serves as the specific blockchain infrastructure where the rewards and smart contracts of JuanDerQuest are deployed to ensure cheap and fast transactions for users.

**Blockchain Reward System.** This refers to a decentralized system that automatically gives digital assets to users when they finish certain tasks. In this study, it is the backend feature that sends digital tokens to tourists immediately after they complete an eco-quest.

**Community-Based Tourism (CBT).** Conceptually, this refers to a tourism approach that allows local communities to actively participate in and benefit from tourism development. Operationally, it serves as the tourism framework within JuanDerQuest where local residents contribute to and benefit from eco-tourism activities and community projects.

**Decentralized.** A system structure where control and data are not held in one single central place or by one single authority. In this study, it describes how tourist achievements and rewards are recorded across a shared blockchain network to prevent data tampering.

**Decentralized Autonomous Organization (DAO).** Conceptually, this refers to a blockchain-based governance structure that allows members to participate in decision-making through transparent voting mechanisms. Operationally, it serves as the governance component of JuanDerQuest that enables community members to vote on proposals and fund allocation decisions.

**Digital Wallet.** Conceptually, this refers to a software application used to store, send, and receive digital assets securely. Operationally, it serves as the user's account within JuanDerQuest where earned JDQ tokens and digital rewards are stored and managed.

**Eco-Quest.** A gamified task or activity centered on environmental protection and cultural appreciation. Operationally, these are the specific location-based challenges inside the application that tourists must complete in Pangasinan, such as visiting an eco-tourism site or participating in a local cleanup.

**Eco-Tourism.** Responsible travel to natural areas that conserves the environment and improves the well-being of local people. In this study, it refers to the community-based tourism activities in Pangasinan that the application aims to support and promote.

**Environmental Stewardship.** Conceptually, this refers to the responsible use, management, and protection of natural resources through sustainable practices. Operationally, it represents the environmental behavior encouraged by JuanDerQuest through conservation-oriented quests and community activities.

**Gamification.** The practice of integrating game elements, like points, challenges, and rewards, into non-game activities to increase user engagement. In this project, it is the method used to motivate tourists to participate in sustainable behaviors by turning eco-tourism tasks into fun activities.

**Geographic Information System (GIS).** Conceptually, this refers to a computer-based system used to collect, store, analyze, and visualize geographic and spatial data. Operationally, it is used in JuanDerQuest to manage destination mapping, quest locations, and location-based tourism information.

**Global Positioning System (GPS).** Conceptually, this refers to a satellite-based navigation technology that determines the precise geographic location of a device. Operationally, it is used by JuanDerQuest to verify user locations and validate the completion of location-based eco-quests.

**Image Recognition.** Conceptually, this refers to a technology that enables computer systems to identify and interpret objects, locations, or activities within digital images. Operationally, it is used to assist in validating photographic evidence submitted by users during quest completion.

**JDQ Token.** Conceptually, this refers to a blockchain-based utility token that serves as a digital reward within a decentralized ecosystem. Operationally, it is the native token distributed to users of JuanDerQuest after successfully completing verified eco-tourism activities.

**Leaderboard.** Conceptually, this refers to a ranking system that displays users according to their performance, achievements, or accumulated points. Operationally, it is the feature within JuanDerQuest that ranks participants based on completed quests and earned rewards.

**Mobile Application.** Conceptually, this refers to software specifically designed to operate on smartphones and mobile devices. Operationally, it serves as the primary platform through which users access the features and services of JuanDerQuest.

**NFT Achievement Badge System.** A system that uses Non-Fungible Tokens (unique digital certificates stored on a blockchain) to represent milestones or achievements. Operationally, these are the digital badges given to tourists as permanent, unchangeable proof that they completed specific eco-quests.

**Smart Contract.** A self-executing digital agreement with the terms of the deal written directly into lines of code on a blockchain network. In this project, smart contracts are used to automatically check quest completions and distribute rewards without needing a middleman.

**Sustainable Tourism.** Conceptually, this refers to tourism that balances environmental protection, economic development, and social well-being to ensure long-term sustainability. Operationally, it describes the tourism model promoted by JuanDerQuest through environmentally responsible and community-centered activities.

**Tokenization.** Conceptually, this refers to the process of converting value, assets, or rights into digital tokens on a blockchain network. Operationally, it is the mechanism through which user participation and environmental contributions are transformed into JDQ token rewards.

**Tourism.** Conceptually, this refers to the activities of individuals traveling to destinations outside their usual environment for leisure, business, or other purposes. Operationally, it refers to the movement and participation of tourists engaging with destinations and activities promoted through JuanDerQuest.

**Tourist Destination.** Conceptually, this refers to a place that attracts visitors because of its natural, cultural, historical, or recreational significance. Operationally, it refers to the attractions, eco-parks, cultural sites, and heritage locations featured within the JuanDerQuest platform.

**Utility Token.** Conceptually, this refers to a digital asset that provides access to specific products, services, or benefits within a blockchain ecosystem. Operationally, it refers to the JDQ token used within JuanDerQuest to reward participation and facilitate ecosystem interactions.

**Web3.** Conceptually, this refers to the next generation of the internet that utilizes blockchain technology, decentralization, and digital ownership. Operationally, it refers to the decentralized technologies integrated into JuanDerQuest, including blockchain rewards, tokenized incentives, digital wallets, and community governance systems.
